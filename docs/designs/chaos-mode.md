## Problem Statement

Chess Royale's turn-based modes serialize every move through `ActiveColor`, one in-flight request, turn completion, and check/checkmate evaluation. Chaos should reuse the same room, board, pieces, avatars, and rematch lifecycle while becoming a distinct real-time VR game:

- both players can move whenever one of their pieces is available;
- pieces retain chess movement geometry but not pins or self-check restrictions;
- capturing the opposing king wins;
- successful moves put only the moved piece on cooldown;
- piece types feel physically different while being dragged; and
- the match is played from ground level, where pieces block sight and players teleport around the board.

The central engineering problem is not removing the turn check. It is giving two simultaneous players one fair, authoritative board while pieces are being held, released, captured, cooled down, or moved toward the same square.

## What Makes This Cool

Chaos turns the giant board from a representation of chess into a place the players inhabit. A queen visibly strains behind the controller, a threatened king can escape during the same instant an attacker lands, and two pieces racing toward one square physically bounce away. The player watches the opponent's avatar and hands instead of waiting for a turn indicator.

The mode stays recognizably chess because pieces still use their normal movement shapes and occupy discrete squares. It becomes its own game because tempo is carried by 32 independent cooldowns rather than one alternating turn.

## Decisions

- Chaos is a focused preset, not a collection of independent switches.
- It forces Standard layout, Ground arena, no Overview, no Three-check, no turn clock, no castling, no en passant, and automatic queen promotion.
- There is no overall match timer in the first release. The match is sudden death until a king is captured, a player disconnects beyond the existing recovery window, or both kings are captured in one authority batch.
- Capturing the enemy king wins. Check is an audiovisual warning and never prevents a move.
- Movement keeps piece geometry, path blocking, friendly occupancy, pawn direction, pawn capture direction, and promotion. Pins, self-check, attacked-king-square restrictions, checkmate, stalemate, repetition, insufficient-material, and fifty-move draws do not apply.
- A player may carry only one piece at a time, even with two controllers.
- A held piece remains logically on its origin square. A translucent origin ghost remains targetable for roughly three seconds while the held visual follows the controller.
- Capturing the origin ghost captures the held piece and cancels that player's pending release. Carry timeout, invalid release, authority handoff, or disconnect returns the piece without a normal cooldown.
- Normal type cooldowns start only after a committed move. First balance values are pawn 2 seconds, knight/bishop 3 seconds, rook 4 seconds, queen 6 seconds, and king 2 seconds. A valid release rejected by destination contention receives a separate one-second jam penalty.
- A cooling piece cannot be grabbed but remains on the board and can be captured.
- Weight is a speed-limited held visual connected to the controller by a short tether. Direct grabs chase the hand; ray grabs chase the controller ray's board-plane target so long rook, bishop, and queen moves do not require a room-scale walk. Release uses the held model's actual square, not the hand or ray target.
- Authority groups releases into 100 ms windows. Nonconflicting moves commit together. Multiple pieces claiming one destination all return home and receive a one-second jam cooldown.
- If a destination occupant has a successful move in the same batch, it escapes. Otherwise the arriving enemy captures it. Two kings captured in one batch produce a mutual-capture draw.
- World-space feedback stays near the affected object: origin ghost and carry timer, hand tether, piece cooldown ring, collision flash, and king-threat warning. Chaos does not add a permanent HUD.

## Premises

1. The existing Fusion state authority remains the only writer of official board state.
2. Physical carry motion is presentation. Official movement remains a discrete origin-to-destination action.
3. The board state at the start of a 100 ms batch is the common input for every release in that batch.
4. Authority tick deadlines, not client clocks, determine carry expiry and cooldown completion. Lease and cooldown eligibility are recorded when authority receives the release, not when the batch later closes.
5. Every authority request includes `RoundSequence`; delayed requests from an old round are rejected.
6. Sprint 5's match configuration, phase, seats, replicated piece IDs, avatars, ground teleporting, results, and rematch agreement remain shared.
7. Turn-based behavior must remain unchanged when `GameplayMode == TurnBased`.
8. Quest and Unity Editor must use matching builds because the Fusion network schema changes.

## Approaches Considered

### Rejected: Mode branches in existing controllers

Add Chaos conditionals directly to `ChessNetworkGameState`, `ChessGameController`, and `ChessPieceInteractable`. This ships fastest but crosses the architecture-upgrade trigger recorded in the Sprint 5 design: Chaos changes move validation, turn completion, outcome evaluation, networking, and interaction at once.

### Selected: Focused rules and interaction policies

Keep one board and one network authority, but extract shared movement geometry and delegate mode-specific decisions to a turn-based or Chaos policy. A Chaos batch resolver owns the 100 ms release window. A separate interaction policy owns carry eligibility, lease duration, cooldown duration, and follow speed.

### Rejected for now: New deterministic simulation core

Move every rule and board mutation into a Unity-independent fixed-tick engine. This is the cleanest base for replays, bots, and many future modes, but it would migrate all working Standard, Chess960, Three-check, promotion, and presentation behavior before Chaos becomes playable.

## Recommended Architecture

### Shared board and movement geometry

`ChessGameController` remains the owner of the piece registry, square lookup, board occupancy, snapshot import/export, presentation, and reset. Extract movement-shape generation from the current legal-move path into `ChessMoveGeometry`:

- pawn forward movement and diagonal capture geometry;
- knight offsets;
- bishop, rook, and queen rays with path blocking;
- king one-square movement; and
- friendly destination rejection.

`TurnBasedRulesPolicy` adds active-color enforcement, self-check simulation, castling, en passant, turn completion, clocks, and existing outcome evaluation. `ChaosRulesPolicy` uses the same geometry but omits turn ownership, self-check, castling, and en passant; it adds automatic queen promotion and king-capture outcomes.

Use an `IChessModeRules` boundary with responsibilities equivalent to:

```text
CanBeginMove(board, piece, seat, authorityTick)
GetDestinations(board, piece)
Resolve(board, moveIntent or intentBatch)
EvaluateOutcome(board, resolvedBatch)
BuildStatus(board, localSeat)
```

The interface describes behavior, not networking. `ChessNetworkGameState` chooses the policy from the immutable `ChessMatchConfig.GameplayMode` and remains responsible for sender identity, round identity, authority, replicated state, and rejection delivery.

### Interaction policy

Use a separate `IChessInteractionPolicy` so VR mechanics do not leak into chess rules:

```text
CanGrab(piece, seat, authorityTick)
CarryDurationTicks(piece)
CooldownDurationTicks(pieceType)
MaximumFollowSpeed(pieceType)
UsesOriginGhost
```

`TurnBasedInteractionPolicy` preserves the current instantaneous grab transformer and one-move request flow. `ChaosInteractionPolicy` supplies the carry lease, ghost, tether, weighted follower, and cooldown gates.

`ChessPieceInteractable` asks the selected interaction policy whether grabbing is allowed and delegates held-position calculation to `ChessPieceGrabTransformer`. It does not contain `if (chaos)` branches for rule validation or victory.

### Network state

Bump `ChessMatchConfig.SchemaVersion` and accept `ChessGameplayMode.Chaos` only when its forced preset fields are canonical. Authority rejects a malformed combination instead of silently changing it after the room opens.

Also bump the Photon/Fusion application protocol version used for matchmaking. Incompatible builds must fail session discovery or join before spawning and deserializing changed network objects; `SchemaVersion` remains a second validation layer after compatible clients connect.

Add compact replicated Chaos state to `ChessNetworkGameState`:

```text
PieceCooldownEndTick[32]
WhiteCarry / BlackCarry:
  Active, PieceId, OriginSquare, Hand, InteractionMode,
  ClientGrabNonce, ExpiresAtTick, CarrySequence
ChaosBatchSequence
```

Cooldowns replicate as end ticks, not decreasing floats. Every client computes the displayed remainder from the authority simulation tick, so the network does not send timer updates each frame.

Only one carry record exists per seat. Beginning a second carry is rejected. Authority handoff cancels both carry records and returns their visuals to the unchanged logical origins; the new authority never guesses an in-flight release.

### Carry flow

```text
Local grab
  → predict held visual and request carry lease with a client grab nonce
  → authority validates seat, ownership, cooldown, origin, round, and free carry slot
  → replicated carry state acknowledges the nonce, creates the origin ghost, and creates the remote held visual
  → local weighted follower chases the direct hand or ray-board target
  → release submits destination, authority-observed carry sequence, and local release sample
  → authority queues valid release for the next 100 ms batch
  → result snapshot commits move/capture/cooldown or returns the visual
```

The client may predict the grab immediately, but it cannot send an authoritative release until the replicated carry acknowledges its `ClientGrabNonce`. If the player releases earlier, the coordinator buffers the model's sampled destination and sends it immediately after acknowledgement. A rejected or timed-out acknowledgement discards the buffered release and returns the model. The three-second lease starts at authority acceptance, so acknowledgement latency consumes a small, visible portion of the lease. The displayed timer uses the replicated authority deadline and never pretends the client received the full interval.

The remote held visual derives its target from the corresponding replicated avatar hand. Direct mode uses hand position; ray mode intersects the replicated controller orientation with the board plane. This is presentation only; the origin ghost is the authoritative target. The carry record includes left/right hand and interaction mode so the opponent sees the correct gesture without a second continuous-position stream.

If authority rejects the lease, the predicted local held visual returns immediately. If an opponent captures the ghost, the interaction manager cancels the local selection before the captured object is hidden, preventing a stale selected object from remaining attached to the controller.

### Batch resolution

Authority windows are anchored to Fusion simulation ticks: `window = floor(receiptTick / ticksPerWindow)`, where `ticksPerWindow = ceil(Runner.TickRate * 0.1)`. A release belongs to the window containing the tick when authority receives it. This removes packet-order bias inside a window, but releases on opposite sides of a boundary are not treated as simultaneous; the first release may still change the next window's board.

At the end of every window, authority resolves all queued releases against one frozen board snapshot:

1. On release receipt, reject stale round, carry-sequence, origin, ownership, cooldown, already-expired lease, invalid-destination, blocked-path, friendly-occupied, and duplicate requests. Record `ReleaseReceivedTick` and clear the carry from further local input. An intent validly queued before lease expiry remains eligible even if the lease deadline passes before the window closes.
2. Group valid intents by destination. Any group with more than one intent is contested; all pieces in that group return and receive the jam cooldown.
3. Build the successful leaver set from the remaining uncontested intents.
4. For each successful intent, an opposing destination occupant escapes if it is in the successful leaver set. Otherwise it is captured.
5. Apply all captures, moves, automatic promotions, normal cooldown deadlines, and carry clears as one batch. A pawn that promotes uses the resulting queen's six-second cooldown, preventing an immediate queen follow-up.
6. If exactly one king was captured, that king's opponent wins. If both were captured, publish `DrawMutualKingCapture`.
7. Publish one snapshot and increment `ChaosBatchSequence` once, even if several moves committed.

This permits swaps and movement cycles when every involved destination is uncontested. It also means two attackers targeting the same king square collide and the king survives that batch.

### Lobby and match presentation

Add a top-level `CLASSIC / CHAOS` gameplay row to the existing world-space Configure page.

- Classic shows the existing Standard/Chess960, Three-check, Podium & Overview, and color choices.
- Chaos replaces the incompatible rows with a read-only preset summary and retains the color choice.
- Create/Wait and guest Confirm show `Chaos · Real-time · King capture · Ground`.

During play, hide the turn clocks and turn label. Use a compact `Protect your king` status, escalating to a spatial warning near the threatened king. Result copy distinguishes king capture, mutual capture, disconnect, and rematch waiting.

### Editor opponent path

Extend `ChessNetworkMovePanel` so a Mac Editor client can participate without VR grabbing. The panel exposes separate **Begin Carry** and **Release** actions that use the same lease RPC, nonce acknowledgement, carry sequence, timeout, batch, and cooldown validation as VR. It bypasses only controller motion and weighted-follow presentation. Optional **Wait**, **Cancel**, and **Let Expire** actions exercise ghost capture, release-before-ack buffering, carry expiry, and stale-carry rejection.

Show each piece's remaining cooldown in the Editor panel and report collision, expiry, stale round, and occupied-friendly-square rejection reasons. This preserves the one-headset black-box workflow while headset-to-headset testing remains the final interaction check.

## Failure and Edge Cases

- Invalid release or release back to origin clears the carry and returns the visual without normal cooldown.
- A carry that expires before release returns without normal cooldown. Repeated timeout abuse is visible but does not create a board advantage because the origin remains occupied and capturable.
- A release received before lease expiry remains valid through its assigned batch even when the batch closes after expiry.
- Releasing before lease acknowledgement buffers one sampled destination locally. Lease rejection or acknowledgement timeout discards it; successful acknowledgement submits it once with the official carry sequence.
- A player cannot grab a cooling piece, a captured piece, the opponent's piece, or a second piece while already carrying one.
- A captured carried piece cancels any later release carrying the old `CarrySequence`.
- A move queued before cooldown expiry is rejected even if its batch resolves after expiry; eligibility is checked at release receipt, preventing early submission.
- A move legal at grab time can be rejected at release time if a nonmoving friendly piece now occupies the destination or its path is blocked in the batch-start board.
- If a destination occupant successfully leaves in the same batch, it cannot be captured at its origin.
- If its intended move collides and bounces, it did not leave and can be captured by an uncontested attacker in that batch.
- Two claims on any one destination collide, including an occupied king square.
- Both kings captured in one batch produce a draw; packet order never chooses the winner.
- Promotion is applied after movement in the same batch, always selects queen in the first release, and applies the queen's six-second cooldown.
- Disconnect or authority handoff never moves a carried piece because logical occupancy stays at origin until commit.
- If state authority changes before a batch closes, the nonreplicated intent queue is discarded, replicated carries are cancelled, and affected clients return to origin. The new authority never replays a partially observed batch.
- If a king-capture batch commits on the same authority tick that disconnect recovery expires, the committed board batch is evaluated first. King capture or mutual capture sets the outcome; disconnect applies only if the match is still in progress afterward.
- Player avatars never block chess movement or participate in move legality. If a committed move places a giant piece onto a player's current anchor, or both players arrive at the same anchor, use a brief comfort fade and relocate each affected rig to the nearest safe anchor on its own side. Seat identity breaks equal-distance ties so both clients choose different fallbacks.
- Rematch clears carry records, cooldown ticks, queued intents, batch sequence presentation, outcomes, and warnings before the next round becomes playable.
- Turn-based modes never allocate Chaos ghosts, carry visuals, cooldown rings, or batch queues.

## Black-Box Verification Plan

No automated test suite is required for this work. Verification uses the existing Unity Editor plus one Quest, followed by a two-headset interaction pass when a second headset is available.

### Unity Editor checks

- Classic Standard, Chess960, and Three-check still start, move, promote, finish, and rematch through their current immediate path.
- Chaos configuration forces and displays the canonical preset on host and guest.
- Editor move panel and Quest can submit nonconflicting moves inside one window and both clients receive one identical batch.
- Two moves targeting the same square bounce and receive the jam cooldown on both clients.
- A destination occupant that leaves in the same batch escapes; one whose move collides remains capturable.
- Old round, old carry, duplicate, wrong-seat, cooling, blocked, friendly-occupied, and expired requests are visibly rejected.
- Releasing before carry acknowledgement buffers exactly one destination, submits once after acknowledgement, and returns safely if the lease is rejected.
- One king capture wins; two king captures in one batch draw; check alone never ends or blocks play.
- A promoting pawn becomes a queen and receives the queen cooldown on both clients.
- Rematch clears all transient Chaos state.
- Disconnect during carry, reconnect inside the recovery window, recovery expiry, and authority handoff with a queued release all return or preserve pieces according to the recovery rules without stale visuals.
- King capture committed on the disconnect-expiry tick takes precedence over disconnect outcome; disconnect wins only if the match remains active.

### On-headset checks

- The local held model visibly trails the controller by piece type without exceeding a comfortable separation.
- The tether reads as weight rather than tracking failure.
- Local and remote players see the correct origin ghost, held model, hand, timer, and cancellation.
- Capturing a held piece safely cancels selection and never leaves a model attached to the controller.
- Cooldown rings are readable from normal ground positions without becoming visual clutter.
- Dynamic teleport anchors cover every currently empty board square plus the perimeter. Capsule checks hide unsafe anchors, and a bounded one-square reach assist lets a player select an adjacent piece without entering its collider.
- Direct and ray grabs can move a piece to every legal destination without teleporting while holding; ray targets travel across the board plane while the weighted model follows at its limited speed.
- Every one of the 64 squares is reachable under representative central blockers, dense pawn walls, and major-piece clusters.
- Moving a piece onto the player's anchor triggers a comfortable fade and relocation to the nearest safe anchor, without affecting the accepted move.
- Two players teleporting to the same anchor are separated through deterministic seat-based fallback anchors without visible clipping or divergent board state.
- Collision, check, capture, timeout, and cooldown-ready feedback are distinguishable through visuals, sound, and haptics.
- Quest 3 sustains 72 FPS for a five-minute full-board Chaos match with all 32 cooldown indicators available and two carry visuals active.

## Success Criteria

- Both players can complete accepted moves during the same 100 ms authority window.
- Clients never disagree about occupancy, cooldown, carry state, capture, or outcome after a published batch.
- Requests received in the same authority window use collision rules independent of their order inside that window. Batch-boundary latency remains observable and is measured during Quest-to-Editor tuning rather than described as fully latency-neutral.
- Holding a piece never removes its authoritative capture target or protects the king.
- Players can identify why a piece cannot move without opening a menu.
- A Mac Editor player can exercise every authoritative Chaos rule against one Quest.
- Existing turn-based modes behave exactly as they did before the policy extraction.
- A new APK built from the same commit as the Editor completes the full black-box checklist.

## Distribution Plan

Chaos ships through the existing Unity Android build and GitHub pull-request flow. The Fusion schema change requires a new APK. Mixed old/new clients are separated by the Photon/Fusion matchmaking protocol version before network objects spawn, then checked again by `SchemaVersion`. No separate package, scene, or application is introduced.

## Implementation Sequence

1. Extract shared `ChessMoveGeometry`, `IChessModeRules`, and `IChessInteractionPolicy`; route current turn-based play through policies without changing behavior.
2. Add the canonical Chaos configuration and world-space lobby summary, while keeping Chaos start disabled until authority state is ready.
3. Add authority-tick cooldown state, nonce-acknowledged one-carry-per-seat leases, buffered early release, origin ghosts, carry timeout, and safe cancellation.
4. Add the direct/ray weighted follower, tether, dynamic empty-square teleport anchors, and bounded reach assist with conservative limits; keep these values data-driven for headset tuning.
5. Add `ChaosBatchResolver`, frozen-board conflict rules, atomic application, automatic queen promotion, king-capture outcomes, and mutual-capture draw.
6. Add spatial cooldown, collision, and check feedback plus result/rematch copy.
7. Extend the Editor move panel, build a matching APK, and run the black-box verification plan.

Do not begin by implementing all 32-piece presentation effects. The first playable vertical slice should use both kings, one pawn, one rook, and one queen per side. It must prove carry leases, ghost capture, weighted following, simultaneous batching, collision, cooldown, and king capture before the full board is enabled.

## Open Tuning Questions

- Confirm the three-second carry lease on headset; shorten it if holding becomes a denial tactic.
- Tune maximum follow speed and maximum hand-to-piece separation per type. The queen must feel heavy without appearing disconnected from tracking.
- Confirm the 100 ms window against real Quest-to-Editor latency; keep fairness fixed and adjust only within a narrow range.
- Decide after playtesting whether stalled matches need an overall time limit. Sudden death is the first-release default.

## The Assignment

Build and black-box the eight-piece vertical slice before enabling the full starting position. Record three facts from a Quest-versus-Editor match: whether players understood the origin ghost without explanation, whether the queen's lag felt like weight rather than tracking failure, and whether a contested-square bounce felt fair. Do not tune the full roster until those three interactions work.

## What I Noticed

- You started with “all these variations should build off of a modular architecture,” and when offered the fastest branch-heavy implementation you chose the policy boundary instead.
- You consistently chose rules players can read in the arena: king capture, an origin ghost, fixed cooldowns, a visible tether, and collision bounce.
- You kept Chaos focused instead of turning every rule into another lobby switch. That gives the mode a distinct identity and keeps the VR panel usable.
